Course Description

This course covers advanced research topics in computer vision with an emphasis on learning structured representations and embeddings. Approaches for learning from unimodal (e.g., images and videos), and multimodal data (e.g., vision and language, vision and audio) will be covered and include topics from structured predications, deep learning and others. The course will be a mix of lecture, student presentation and discussion.

Course Objectives: PhD Students: gain research experience in computer vision; MS Students: get prepared for a software engineer career in computer vision and artificial intelligence.

  • Get exposed to a variety of computer vision topics and be familiar with state-of-the-arts
  • Learn to critically evaluate other peoples' work
  • Propose and implement a research-oriented computer vision project.

Prerequisites: Machine Vision or Machine Learning or Deep Learning or permission of the instructor.

Course Work and Evaluation

  • Paper Critics (25%): Throughout the term, we will read many papers. You are asked to write paper critics for a subset of them.
  • Paper Presentations (25%): You are expected to present a few papers over the term. Grading is based on the preparedness and clarity of your presentations.
  • Course Project (25%): You will work on a semester-long research-oriented computer vision project. There will be a project proposal one-third down the course, a midway status presentation, and a final project presentation. You are expected to produce a mini research paper by the end of the term.
  • Class Participation (25%): Attend every class and actively participate in class discussions. Don't be shy to speak!
