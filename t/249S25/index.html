Course Description

Fundamentals of computer vision, including image formation, elements of human vision, low-level image processing, and pattern recognition techniques. Advanced topics include modern visual features, graphical models, model-based and data-driven approaches, and contextual inference, as well as illustrative examples of successes and challenges in applications. CSC 449, a graduate-level course, requires additional readings and assignments.

Rough Topic Outline:
  • Image Formation and Multiple View Geometry: camera models, camera calibration, image alignment, structure from motion
  • Features and Filters: image transforms, linear filtering, edges, corners, image scale space, feature extraction, feature matching, optical flow
  • Segmentation and Fitting: Gestalt principles, segmentation by clustering, image as graphs, robust fitting and RANSAC, Hough transform
  • Recognition: feature learning, convolutional and recurrent neural networks, advanced CNN architectures, attention mechanisms, feature visualization, image classification, object detection, instance segmentation, scene parsing, image captioning, face recognition, style transfer, generative image models

Course Objective: An introduction to computer vision. The course is designed as an upper-level elective for Computer Science undergraduate students and an AI-area breath course for graduate students. The students will develop a strong understanding of formulating and solving problems using computer vision.

Course Work and Evaluation

  • Attendance and Participation (10%): Throughout the term, we will have a few attendance takings. They are equally weighted. The instructor will note students who actively participate in class discussion. These together count 10% toward the final letter grade.
  • Exam (30%): We will have an in-class midterm exam. It will have primarily multiple choice questions.
  • Problem Sets (30%): There will be two problem sets (PS) over the term. They contain a mix of analytical and programming questions and are to be done independently by each student.
  • Course Project (30%): Students will form groups with up to two members to implement a course project, which will generate a project report and a presentation at the end of the term.

Late Work Policy: Deduct 1% total score of the late assignment per hour passing the deadline. E.g., you submit PS-1 12 hours passing the deadline, then the max score you will get is 88%. We will not accept your submission after 72 hours over the deadline. All assignments should be submitted on Blackboard. You have plenty of time to do course assignments. Please arrange your time well; no late work exceptions will be given.

Regrading: Any questions about the grading of a piece of work must be raised within one week of the date that the work was returned. In other words, if you do not pick up your work in a timely fashion, you may forfeit your right to question the grading of your work.
